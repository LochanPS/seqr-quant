## The novel result: passive measurement misses 60 points of PQC

Passive sslyze reports **0%** PQC. Active probing (sending an ML-KEM-capable
ClientHello) confirms **60%**. The gap is explained entirely by the edge:

| Edge provider | Hybrid PQC confirmed (active) |
|---|---|
| Amazon CloudFront | 18 / 19 (95%) |
| Cloudflare | 14 / 16 (88%) |
| Direct origin (no CDN) | **0 / 18 (0%)** |

Endpoints passive missed but active confirmed include razorpay.com, payu.in,
phonepe.com, zerodha.com (+ kite/coin), groww.in, cashfree — all CloudFront/
Cloudflare fronted.

## Contributions for the paper

1. **Methodological.** Passive TLS measurement systematically undercounts PQC
   adoption. Every prior "X% PQC" measurement study that used passive scanning is
   a lower bound; the true edge figure can be ~60 points higher. Active probing is
   required for accurate PQC measurement.
2. **Empirical.** Indian fintech PQC adoption is real (~60% at the edge) but is
   **entirely CDN-driven**, not origin-driven. Every directly-hosted origin is 100%
   classical. PQC arrived as a free CloudFront/Cloudflare default, not a deliberate
   migration by the fintechs themselves.

## Honest caveats (must appear in the paper)

- Edge hybrid PQC protects the **client↔edge** hop only, NOT edge↔origin.
- The hybrid is **key exchange** (X25519MLKEM768). Server **authentication**
  (certificates/signatures) is still 100% classical (RSA/ECDSA) — quantum-
  vulnerable to forgery once CRQCs exist. "60% PQC" ≠ "60% quantum-safe."
- Origins remain 100% classical; the orgs have done no inventory/migration.
- Data terminating at a foreign-operated CDN edge has its own DPDP / data-
  sovereignty implications worth noting.

## Product implication

This is the live demonstration of SeQr's active-probe differentiator: a passive
scanner (and most competitors) would tell an Indian fintech "you have no PQC." The
truth is more useful — "your edge has PQC, but your certs, your origin, and your
CBOM do not, and that's what PCI DSS / SEBI CSCRF / DST ask for."

## Origin exposure (new finding — `research/origin_leakage.py`)

Passive origin discovery (common ancillary subdomains → non-CDN public IPs) across 118
domains: **60 (51%, 95% CI 42–60) expose at least one origin endpoint outside the CDN.**

| Region | Exposed origin | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| **All** | 60/118 (51%) | 42–60 |
| India | 45/94 (48%) | 38–58 |
| Comparison (US/EU/UK/SG/BR) | 15/24 (62%) | 43–79 |

Examples: `api.`, `dev.`, `sftp.`, `gateway.`, `staging.` resolving to direct cloud IPs.
**Implication:** edge PQC is often illusory — for half the corpus a classical origin is
directly reachable outside the protected edge, so an adversary bypasses the edge entirely.
None of the discovered origins served a PQC certificate. (Aggregate only; per-entity
weakness data requires responsible disclosure before publication.)
